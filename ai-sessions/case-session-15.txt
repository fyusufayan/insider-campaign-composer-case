  - What I added to the documented contract:
    - expected_version on confirm and withdraw.
    - The GET route, which reloads use to restore the dialog.
    - active_approval_id on the campaign response.
  - Strict JSON: request bodies are capped at 64 KiB; unknown or duplicate
    properties and non-integer numbers get 422; the wrong media type gets 415.
  - Errors:
    - 404 for an unknown approval, or one belonging to another campaign.
    - 409 version_conflict, invalid_state, approval_expired or
      approval_mismatch; a mismatch lists the differing fields.
    - 422 validation_failed or empty_audience.
    - 409 audience_changed and 503 campaign_busy, both marked retryable.
    - A plain 500 for anything unexpected, with no SQL or customer data in the
      message.
  - Privacy: responses never contain user ids, names, cities, contacts, frozen
    personalization values, destinations or idempotency keys.

  2. Confirm and withdraw transactions (approval/decision.go). Each one locks
  the campaign row, then the target approval, then applies expiry under that
  lock using the database clock. If the approval turns out to be due, the expiry
  is committed and the request returns approval_expired; it is never rolled
  back with the rejected request.
  - Confirm checks, in order:
    - the approval's own status;
    - the expected version and campaign state;
    - the binding (segment version, content version, channels), against both the
      request and the campaign;
    - a fresh content review against the current brief;
    - the held rows: per-channel counts and digest must match what was stored.

    It then promotes the approval to approved and moves the campaign to
    approved. Held rows and content are untouched.
  - Withdraw marks the approval revoked (withdrawn), deletes its held rows
    (their count must equal the stored snapshot count) and returns the campaign
    to content_ready.

  3. States and versions. Every real transition raises the campaign version by
  exactly one: prepare, confirm, withdraw and expiry. Of two simultaneous
  confirms, one wins and the other gets invalid_state; of confirm racing
  withdraw, exactly one applies. Sending and finished campaigns are never
  changed, even when their used approval's old expiry time has passed. Lazy
  expiry now also runs, committed, before campaign reads and edits, so an edit
  based on the pre-expiry view gets a 409.

  4. TTLs. A pending approval expires 15 minutes after preparation. An approved
  one expires 24 hours after confirmation (approved_at); the 15-minute limit
  never applies to it. Both are configurable (APPROVAL_PENDING_TTL, 1s–24h;
  APPROVAL_APPROVED_TTL, 1s–7 days), along with APPROVER_NAME, in .env.example
  and Compose.

  5. Offer findings. The documented contract has no acknowledgement field, so I
  did not invent one (ADR-023). Prepare, read and confirm each recompute the
  findings from the stored content and the current brief. The dialog lists each
  finding individually, says the check is keyword-based and advisory, and
  requires ticking an acknowledgement, plus a second one when there are
  findings. That checkbox is a UI control only; the server-side guarantee is the
  version-and-binding check.

  6. Approval UI. A new step 5 offers Prepare approval, and says why it is
  unavailable (unsaved or invalid draft, no channel). The review dialog shows:
  - per-channel frozen recipients with no-consent/no-contact counts and reach;
  - content version and origin, segment version and snapshot digest;
  - a countdown and render issues;
  - the rendered previews and the offer findings;
  - an explanation that consent and contact are re-checked at delivery.

  From there you can Confirm approval, Withdraw approval, or Close without 
  confirming. An approved campaign shows a read-only summary, and withdrawing it
  takes a second click. There is no send button. No v-html is used, and
  approval responses are decoded strictly: unknown properties are rejected, with
  cross-field consistency checks.

  7. Frontend concurrency. Late responses after switching campaigns are dropped,
  repeated clicks send one request, and a 409 is shown with a reload path and
  never retried. When the countdown reaches zero, Confirm is disabled and the
  server is asked to re-check. A refresh while pending reopens the dialog; a
  refresh while approved shows the summary.
